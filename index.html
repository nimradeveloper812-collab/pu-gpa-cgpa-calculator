How to Calculate GPA & CGPA at Punjab University (Step-by-Step)

The University of the Punjab (PU) operates under a standard Semester Examination Statute for all undergraduate (BS 4-Year) and graduate (Associate Degree 2-Year) programs. Understanding how your marks convert into Grade Points (GP) and quality points ensures you remain in Good Academic Standing.

1. Quality Points (QP)

QP = Credit Hours × Course Grade Point

For example, 3 Credit Hours with an ‘A’ (4.00 GP) gives: 3 × 4.00 = 12.00 Quality Points.

2. Semester GPA

Semester GPA = ∑ QP ÷ Total Semester Credits

Divide the sum of all quality points earned in the semester by the total registered credit hours.

3. Cumulative CGPA

CGPA = ∑(All QP) ÷ ∑(Total Credits)

PU explicitly mandates that CGPA is computed across all evaluated individual courses, not averaged from semester GPAs.

4. Obtained Percentage Marks

OPM (%) = ∑(Credits × Marks) ÷ Total Credits

The weighted percentage of marks earned across the entire degree program.

Punjab University Marks Ceiling Rule (Section 5):

Under official PU regulations, any fractional mark in a course is automatically rounded up to the immediate next whole integer (‘1’ mark). E.g., 64.1 or 64.9 marks become 65 (Grade B-, 2.70 GP). Similarly, 71.4 becomes 72 (Grade B, 3.00 GP). This calculator natively applies this ceiling rule.

Punjab University Academic Standing, Probation & Drop Rules

Sections 12 and 13 of the Punjab University Examination Code govern student promotion and retention:

1st Semester Examination Thresholds:

• GPA ≥ 2.00: Promoted to 2nd semester in Good Standing.
• 1.50 ≤ GPA < 2.00: Placed on 1st Academic Probation.
• GPA < 1.50: Automatically Dropped from Rolls.

2nd Semester Onwards Thresholds:

• CGPA ≥ 2.00: Promoted in Good Standing.
• 1.70 ≤ CGPA < 2.00: Promoted on Probation (maximum 2 probations allowed in the whole degree).
• CGPA < 1.70: Automatically Dropped from Rolls.

Degree Award Criterion:

A minimum cumulative CGPA of 2.00 out of 4.00 is mandatory for the conferment of a BS 4-Year or Associate Degree at University of the Punjab.

Frequently Asked Questions (PU GPA & CGPA FAQs)

How is GPA calculated at Punjab University (PU)?

At Punjab University, semester GPA is calculated by multiplying each course's credit hours by its earned Grade Points (GP) to obtain Quality Points, adding all Quality Points together, and dividing by total registered semester credit hours: GPA = ∑(Credit Hours × GP) ÷ ∑(Credit Hours).

What is the Punjab University fraction ceiling rule for marks?

Under official PU examination regulations, any fraction of marks in a course is counted as 1 full mark. For example, 64.1 or 64.9 marks are rounded up to 65 (Grade B-, 2.70 GP), and 71.4 marks are rounded up to 72 (Grade B, 3.00 GP).

How is cumulative CGPA computed from multiple semesters at PU?

Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is computed directly from all evaluated courses across all enrolled semesters: CGPA = ∑(All Course Credits × Course GP) ÷ ∑(Total Evaluated Credits). PU examination statutes declare that CGPA is NOT calculated by averaging semester GPAs.

What is the minimum CGPA required to graduate from Punjab University?

A minimum cumulative CGPA of 2.00 out of 4.00 is required for the conferment of both 4-year Bachelor's degrees (BS) and 2-year Associate degrees (ADP) at Punjab University.

What happens if a student receives an 'F' or 'D' grade?

An ‘F’ grade (below 50% marks) has 0.00 Grade Points and requires mandatory repetition of the course. A ‘D’ grade (50–54% marks, 1.00 GP) is a conditional low pass; repeating the course is mandatory only if the student’s cumulative CGPA is below 2.00.
